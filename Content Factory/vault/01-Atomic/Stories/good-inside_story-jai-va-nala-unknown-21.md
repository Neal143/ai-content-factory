<situation>Jai is a six-year-old who doesn’t enjoy playing in large groups. At a gymnastics birthday party, Jai hides behind his mom, Nala, while the other kids separate and start playing on the equipment.</situation>
<problem>In a soft, low voice, Nala says, “Jai, you’re six and you know every single kid here! You’re being ridiculous!” Jai starts crying, and Nala gets frustrated.</problem>
<turning_point>“You’re embarrassing me!” she says. Then Nala feels overwhelmed with guilt. She doesn’t know what to do next.</turning_point>
<outcome>When that happens, the child internalizes their parent’s judgment, which causes them to feel more alone in their emotions and even more stuck in them, which leads to more parental frustration. Then the cycle continues and the hesitance and anxiety intensify.</outcome>
<lesson>Hesitation and shyness are not problems to fix... How do we break this cycle? Well, not by changing a child; we start by reflecting on ourselves and doing the work internally.</lesson>
